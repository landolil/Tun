## 4. FINAL GAS TREATMENT — CO₂ CAPTURE & N₂ RECOVERY

### 4.1 CO₂ Separation via Pressure Swing Adsorption (PSA)

After Train C combustion (Option A) or reforming (Option B), the gas stream consists primarily of N₂ and CO₂ with trace residual gases. CO₂ must be separated for two reasons: (1) to recover high-purity N₂ for recycle loop closure, and (2) to capture CO₂ for reuse as drum purge gas and geopolymer curing atmosphere.

**PSA with Zeolite 13X** is the selected technology:
- Zeolite 13X adsorbs CO₂ preferentially at 3-5 bar; desorbs at 1 bar
- Two-column alternating cycle: 60s adsorption / 30s desorption
- CO₂ purity output: 95-99% (sufficient for inert purge and geopolymer curing)
- N₂ purity output: 97-99.5% (recycled to drum atmosphere)
- Column body: BFRP/Elium® with PEEK CF40 valve manifold
- Valve seats: ZrO₂ ceramic check valves (no metallic contact)

**Recovered CO₂ Uses:**
1. Drum purge gas supplement (both N₂ and CO₂ are inert at operating temperatures)
2. CBPC geopolymer curing atmosphere (CO₂ curing accelerates strength development)
3. Future: CO₂-to-CO via plasma arc for carbon synthesis feedstock

### 4.2 Plasma Torch Polisher — Final Gas Treatment

For any trace organics, halogens, or dioxin/furan precursors that escape all prior trains, a final plasma torch at the gas outlet provides 6-log destruction efficiency.

**Plasma Torch Construction:**
- Electrodes: ZrO₂-coated tungsten (tungsten for 3000°C+ resistance; ZrO₂ coating for corrosion)
- Housing: ZrB₂-SiC ceramic (withstands 3000°C plasma temperatures)
- Power: 15 kW DC (plasma arc temperature: 3000-8000°C)
- Residence time at >1000°C: 0.5 seconds (sufficient for complete organic destruction)
- Dioxin/furan threshold: PCDD/PCDF destruction efficiency > 99.9999% at 1200°C, 2 seconds

The plasma torch is the system's regulatory safety net — ensuring zero toxic organic emissions.
