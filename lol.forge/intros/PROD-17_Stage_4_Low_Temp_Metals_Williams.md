# Williams Heuristic Intro — PROD-17 Stage 4 Low-Temperature Metals
## Constraint, Necessity, Commitment

### Constraint (The Lock)

The irreducible principle forcing this design is **thermodynamic selectivity via melting point hierarchy**. Indium melts at 156.6°C. Tin at 231.9°C. Bismuth at 271.5°C. Lead at 327.5°C. Zinc at 419.5°C. Aluminum at 660.3°C. These are not engineering choices — they are fundamental constants of nature, immutable phase boundaries written in the electron configurations of these elements. No alloying, no flux, no pressure, no clever catalyst can change the fact that at 160°C, indium is the only liquid metal in the feedstock. This thermodynamic constraint is absolute. The design of Stage 4 is not an optimization problem — it is a transcription problem. We must transcribe the melting point sequence into a temperature-time profile that respects each phase boundary. The drum ramp rate, the dwell times, the RPM profile, the port actuation temperatures — every parameter derives from this single constraint: **the melting points are fixed, so the process must conform to them**.

This constraint propagates through every subsystem. The induction frequency (50 kHz) is chosen because skin depth in molten Zn/Pb/Sn/In at 400-500°C matches particle sizes. The centrifugal RPM (400 for In, 300 for Sn/Pb/Bi, 400 for Zn) is chosen because the density differences between molten metals create stable stratification only above a critical G-force. The N₂/H₂ atmosphere at 1.5 bar is chosen because In₂O₃ forms spontaneously at 156°C in air but reduces to In + H₂O in hydrogen. The Ti₃AlC₂/ZrO₂ valve actuators are calibrated to 160°C, 200°C, 280°C, 340°C, 430°C because those are the melting points plus superheat margins. The Na₂CO₃ flux chemistry is chosen because it reduces surface oxides on all four metals at their respective melting temperatures. Nothing in Stage 4 is arbitrary. Every number traces back to the melting point hierarchy.

### Necessity (The Key)

The empirical gap this resolves is **domestic indium supply independence**. The United States imports 100% of its indium — 58% from China, the rest from Korea, Japan, Canada, Belgium. No primary indium mine exists in the US; indium is only recovered as a byproduct of zinc smelting. Global production is ~700 tons/year. CSMFAB's YInMn Blue pigment alone requires ~2 tons/year at scale. The Aegis-Forge I processes ~600,000 kg/year of mixed electronics feedstock containing ~1-2% indium by weight in ITO coatings — a potential recoverable stream of 6-12 tons/year. This exceeds total US consumption. The Indium-First Strategy is not a feature — it is the economic and strategic justification for the entire Stage 4 architecture. Without indium recovery, Stage 4 is merely a solder/zinc/lead recycler — valuable but not strategic. With indium recovery, Stage 4 becomes a **critical mineral production asset** disguised as a thermal separator.

The solution is inevitable because the physics demands it. You cannot recover indium at Stage 5 (660°C) — it would have already alloyed with tin, oxidized, or vaporized. You cannot recover it at Stage 3 (700°C) — it would be lost in the gas phase or dissolved in slag. You must catch it at its melting point, 156.6°C, before any other metal liquefies. This requires a dedicated sub-stage at the bottom of the Stage 4 temperature range, with its own RPM, its own port, its own valve actuation temperature, its own atmosphere control. The 5-minute dwell at 160°C at 400 RPM is not a process parameter — it is the minimum time for indium droplets to coalesce and migrate radially 150mm under 89 G. The electrodeposition refining downstream is not an add-on — it is the only path from 60-80% In slug to 99.9% In cathode. The YInMn Blue connection is not a marketing angle — it is the demand signal that sizes the indium recovery capacity. Every element of the Indium-First Strategy is necessary because the alternative is continued import dependence.

### Commitment (The Turn)

This document makes three non-negotiable commitments:

1. **Temperature fidelity**: The drum will ramp through 155→157→160°C at 1°C/min and hold at 160°C ±1°C for 5 minutes. No acceleration. No compromise. If the temperature control system cannot maintain ±1°C at 160°C, the Indium-First Strategy fails. This commits the PID tuning, the FBG sensor density (384 sensors, Zone 4 density), the induction power resolution (80 kW at 50 kHz), and the thermal model validation (PROD-24) to a standard tighter than any other stage.

2. **Atmosphere integrity**: The N₂/H₂ atmosphere will maintain <1 ppm O₂ and >0.075 bar H₂ partial pressure throughout Stage 4. If oxygen intrudes at 160°C, In₂O₃ forms and indium is lost to slag. If hydrogen drops below 5%, the reducing potential is insufficient. This commits the gas system (PROD-07) to continuous O₂ monitoring, H₂ injection with redundant valves, zirconia getters in the annulus, and emergency N₂ purge at 10 bar on any O₂ excursion.

3. **Port 4A actuation at 160°C**: The Ti₃AlC₂/ZrO₂ bilayer actuator for Port 4A will open at 160°C ±2°C. Not 165°C. Not 155°C. The actuator design (PROD-09, FOLLOW-04) is committed to this calibration. The PEEK CF40 return spring (50 N preload) is committed to holding 5 bar N₂ at 160°C. The ZrO₂ piston (12 mm, 42 g) is committed to sealing against 565 N pressure force plus 50 N spring force until thermal actuation generates ~2,400 N opening force. This is a hardware commitment — the valves are manufactured to this spec, tested to this spec, and cannot be retuned in the field.

These commitments are not aspirational. They are the design basis. If any cannot be met, the Indium-First Strategy is invalid and Stage 4 must be redesigned from first principles.
