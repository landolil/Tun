# Williams Heuristic — PROD-20: Stage 7 Ferrous Cascade
## Architectural Clarity through Constraint, Necessity, and Commitment

### Constraint (The Lock)

The irreducible principle forcing this design is the **ferromagnetic coupling cliff at the Curie temperature (770°C)**. Below 770°C, iron concentrates magnetic flux 100-1000×, giving 2-10× power density for induction heating. Above 770°C, iron becomes paramagnetic (μᵣ=1) and couples only via eddy currents like copper or aluminum. This creates a fundamental thermal discontinuity: the first 770°C of heating (ambient to Curie) is radically more efficient than the remaining 800°C to melting (1538°C). The lock is magnetic: you cannot design Stage 7 induction without exploiting the ferromagnetic regime, but you must also traverse the non-magnetic regime to reach melting. The 2 kHz frequency is the compromise — deep enough for non-magnetic penetration (δ=8.5 mm at 1580°C), low enough to maintain ferromagnetic coupling advantage during the critical first half of the thermal ramp.

The second constraint is the **thermodynamic imperative of REE oxidation by FeO**. Rare earth elements (Nd, Sm, Dy, Y) have higher oxygen affinity than iron. In the Fe-FeO-REE system at 1580°C, the equilibrium Nd + 3FeO ⇌ Nd₂O₃ + 3Fe lies FAR to the right (ΔG° = -180 kJ/mol). This means REEs WILL oxidize and partition to slag if FeO is present. The lock is chemical: you cannot keep REEs in the iron bullion if you have FeO in the slag. But you NEED FeO in the slag for slag fluidity, basicity control, and as the oxidation agent for carbon control. The only escape is partitioning control: direct the REE-rich slag droplets to a separate port (Port 7A) before they equilibrate with the main iron stream. The dual-port architecture is not optional — it is mandated by the REE-Fe-O phase diagram.

The third constraint is the **cascade mass balance closure for magnesium and aluminum oxides**. Stage 4 dross delivers 10 kg MgO, Stage 5 dross delivers 7 kg MgO/Al₂O₃. These MUST go somewhere. They cannot report to Stage 6 (copper would be contaminated). They MUST report to Stage 7 slag. The lock is material: the cascade produces 17 kg of "free" MgO/Al₂O₃ per batch from upstream dross. Stage 7 slag engineering MUST consume this as flux — it is not optional, it is the only thermodynamically consistent destination. The slag basicity (B=1.2) and MgO/Al₂O₃ ratio (1.5) are not design choices — they are the inevitable consequence of upstream mass balance.

### Necessity (The Key)

The empirical gap this resolves is the **recovery of rare earth elements from end-of-life magnets without dedicated hydrometallurgical preprocessing**. Conventional e-waste recycling shreds hard drives and motors, then uses acid leaching to extract Nd/Sm/Co — generating massive acidic waste, losing REEs to complex solution chemistry, and requiring expensive separation trains. The Aegis-Forge Stage 7 closes this gap by using the thermal cascade itself as the REE concentrator: the AMLS (PROD-05) magnetically separates NdFeB/SmCo magnets upstream; Stage 7 melts these magnets IN THE IRON STREAM; the REEs oxidize into the slag phase driven by FeO; the dual-port architecture (Port 7A at 1500°C, inward of Port 7) captures the REE-rich slag/metal droplets BEFORE they mix into the main iron bullion; FOLLOW-15 hydrometallurgy completes the recovery at >90% efficiency. The key insight: the thermal cascade REPLACES the magnet leach plant. The furnace IS the REE concentrator.

The solution is inevitable because **the thermal cascade architecture (PROD-02) creates a ferrous melt that is ALREADY enriched in REEs** before it reaches Stage 7. The AMLS magnetic separation at Stage 0 captures >95% of NdFeB/SmCo magnets. These magnets enter Stage 7 as discrete particles that melt at 1177°C (NdFeB) and 1490°C (SmCo) — well within the Stage 7 window. By the time the main iron melts at 1538°C, the REEs are ALREADY dissolved in the iron matrix. Stage 7 merely provides the slag chemistry (FeO, basicity) and physical separation (dual-port) to extract them. The necessity is architectural: without Stage 7's dual-port REE extraction, the REEs concentrated by AMLS would be lost to the Stage 8 mineral residue.

### Commitment (The Turn)

This document makes three non-negotiable commitments:

**1. The Dual-Port REE Separation Commitment:** Stage 7 WILL produce a separate REE concentrate stream (Port 7A) containing >70% of feed Nd, >70% of feed Sm, >55% of feed Co, and >70% of feed Dy. This is not a target — it is a geometric consequence of the centrifugal equilibrium radii: REE-rich droplets (ρ=7.5-8.0 g/cm³) at r_eq=275-280 mm vs. Fe-rich liquid (ρ=7.0) at r_eq=285 mm. The 30 mm radial separation between Port 7A (r=250mm) and Port 7 (r=280mm) is the physical embodiment of this commitment. Any change to port radii, temperatures, or RPM breaks the REE recovery chain.

**2. The Zero-Purchased-Flux Commitment:** Stage 7 slag engineering uses NO purchased flux (CaO, SiO₂, MgO, Al₂O₃). The CaO/SiO₂ basicity (B=1.2) is achieved by adding only 15 kg CaO to the 35 kg SiO₂ from upstream mineral residue. The MgO/Al₂O₃ (17 kg total) comes ENTIRELY from Stage 4 and 5 dross — waste from upstream stages becomes flux for Stage 7. This commitment flows directly to PROD-21 (Stage 8 slag composition) and PROD-07 (off-gas load). Purchasing flux would be an admission of cascade design failure.

**3. The Carbon Control Commitment:** Stage 7 WILL maintain Fe bullion carbon at 2.0-4.0% (by stream) via CO/CO₂ gas injection ratio control. This is not a metallurgical preference — it is a cascade requirement. Carbon <2% → steel too ductile for castings; carbon >4% → excessive shrinkage, cracking. The CO/CO₂ ratio is controlled in real-time by inline XRF carbon measurement at Port 7 exit, feeding back to mass flow controllers. This commitment locks Stage 7 to PROD-07 (CO/CO₂ supply from pyrolysis gas + PSA) and PROD-19 (Stage 6 off-gas CO recycle).

These three commitments — dual-port REE separation, zero purchased flux, real-time carbon control — are the turns that lock Stage 7 into the thermal cascade architecture. They cannot be negotiated without breaking the REE recovery chain that begins at AMLS (PROD-05) and ends at FOLLOW-15/FOLLOW-33 (magnet manufacturing).
