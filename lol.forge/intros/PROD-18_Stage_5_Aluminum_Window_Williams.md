# Williams Heuristic Intro — PROD-18 Stage 5 Aluminum Window
## Constraint, Necessity, Commitment

### Constraint (The Lock)

The irreducible principle forcing this design is the **60°C Aluminum Window** — the thermodynamic interval between aluminum's liquidus (660.3°C) and magnesium's vaporization onset (720°C). This window is not an engineering choice; it is a fundamental constant written in the phase diagrams of the Al-Mg-Cu-Fe-Si system. At 660°C, aluminum melts. At 720°C, magnesium vapor pressure exceeds 10 Pa and Mg loss becomes significant. Between these two temperatures, and ONLY between them, aluminum can be separated from copper (1085°C) and iron (1538°C) while magnesium remains in the melt for flux removal. The window is narrow, absolute, and non-negotiable. Every parameter in Stage 5 — the 300 RPM, the 10 kHz induction, the NaCl/KCl/cryolite flux, the 720°C Port 5 actuation, the 25-minute dwell — derives from this single constraint: **the process must complete aluminum melting, magnesium removal, and alloy fractionation within the 60°C window before magnesium vaporizes**.

This constraint propagates through every subsystem. The induction frequency (10 kHz) is chosen because skin depth in liquid aluminum at 680°C (δ ≈ 0.9 mm) matches the 1-5 mm aluminum particle sizes in the feedstock. The drum rotation (300 RPM, G=25.1) is chosen because the aluminum melt viscosity (1.3 mPa·s) and film thickness (0.8 mm) produce a drainage velocity (0.15 m/s) that clears the 800 mm drum length in 5 seconds — leaving 25 minutes for magnesium removal. The flux composition (NaCl/KCl 50/50 + 5% cryolite) is chosen because its eutectic (657°C) is fully liquid at 660°C, it dissolves the Al₂O₃ skin that would block Mg oxidation, and its basicity (0.8-1.0) maximizes MgO solubility while minimizing aluminum loss. The Port 5 actuation temperature (720°C ±2°C) is the upper bound of the window — any higher and magnesium vaporizes; any lower and the melt hasn't fully cleared. Nothing in Stage 5 is arbitrary. Every number traces back to the Aluminum Window.

### Necessity (The Key)

The empirical gap this resolves is **aluminum recovery economics in a zero-metal thermal cascade**. The Aegis-Forge I is designed as an all-dielectric, Carrington-immune machine that avoids structural metals in its hot zone. Yet the feedstock contains 60-140 kg of aluminum per 500 kg batch — a massive energy sink and a potential contamination source for downstream stages. If aluminum is not removed at Stage 5, it carries into Stage 6 (copper, 1085°C) where it forms Al-Cu intermetallics that degrade copper conductivity, and into Stage 7 (ferrous, 1200-1600°C) where it reduces to Al₂O₃ slag that dilutes rare earth recovery. The aluminum MUST be removed at Stage 5. But aluminum scrap has value — $1.80-3.00/kg depending on purity. The cascade economics require this value to offset the parasitic energy costs (rotation, gas, cooling) that the all-dielectric architecture cannot avoid. The Aluminum Window is not just a separation step — it is the **economic enabler** that makes the zero-metal architecture financially viable.

The solution is inevitable because the physics demands it. You cannot remove aluminum at Stage 4 (420-900°C) — zinc boils at 907°C and would contaminate the aluminum. You cannot remove it at Stage 6 (1085°C) — by then aluminum has already alloyed with copper and iron, forming intermetallics that cannot be separated. You cannot remove it chemically — chlorine flux is toxic, vacuum distillation is too slow for batch cascade. The ONLY path is the Aluminum Window: heat to 680°C, hold for magnesium removal, drain by centrifugal force, fractionate by liquidus temperature. The 25-minute dwell is not a process parameter — it is the minimum time for 93% magnesium removal via flux reaction with centrifugal surface renewal. The 4+4 mold fractionation is not a convenience — it is the minimum to separate wrought (<0.15% Si) from cast (5-12% Si) alloys for downstream value. Every element of the Stage 5 design is necessary because the alternative is either aluminum contamination of premium copper/REE streams or loss of aluminum revenue that funds the cascade.

### Commitment (The Turn)

This document makes three non-negotiable commitments:

1. **Temperature ceiling at 720°C**: The drum will not exceed 720°C during Stage 5. No just 5 degrees higher for faster melting. No operator override. If the temperature control system cannot maintain ≤720°C, the magnesium removal fails, magnesium vaporizes, and the aluminum product is contaminated with MgO fume. This commits the PID tuning (outer loop 0.6/0.03/0.08), the FBG sensor density (48 sensors in Zones 4-5), the induction power resolution (100 kW at 10 kHz, ZVS tracking), and the thermal model validation (PROD-24) to a standard tighter than any stage except Stage 4's indium window.

2. **Magnesium removal to <0.1% (1000 ppm)**: The NaCl/KCl/cryolite flux will reduce feed magnesium (0.3-1.5%) to <0.1% in the aluminum product. If the flux chemistry cannot achieve this, the wrought stream fails specification (<0.1% Mg), the cast stream exceeds 0.5% Mg, and downstream casting produces hot-short parts. This commits the flux injection system (12 kg/batch, 0.2 kg/min during dwell), the cryolite content (5% minimum, validated by MgF₂ formation thermodynamics), the XRF feedback control (10 sec acquisition, cryolite rate adjustment), and the dross recovery rotary furnace (FOLLOW-20) to a performance standard verified every batch by ICP-OES.

3. **Fractionation by liquidus temperature**: The 13 temperature bands (5 min each, 660-720°C) will be collected to 8 ZrO₂ mold positions with XRF-verified composition. If the fractionation fails, wrought and cast streams mix, wrought Si exceeds 0.15%, cast Si falls below 5%, and both streams lose 30-50% of their market value. This commits the inline XRF (Si-PIN, 50 kV Rh, 10 sec), the mold carousel (8-position, 3-min index), the ZrO₂ mold thermal design (PROD-08: Cu chill for wrought, insulated for cast), and the Scheil segregation model (k_Si=0.12, k_Fe=0.03, k_Cu=0.17) to a fidelity where post-batch assay matches real-time XRF prediction within ±0.05% Si.

These commitments are not aspirational. They are the design basis. If any cannot be met, the Aluminum Window closes and Stage 5 must be redesigned from first principles.
