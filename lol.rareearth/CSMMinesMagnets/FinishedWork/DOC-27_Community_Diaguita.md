## Chapter 1: Diaguita Origin & Valleys

### 1.1 Diaguita Origin and Territorial Identity

The Diaguita people (Diaguita-Kakán linguistic family) are the **pre-Inca, pre-Hispanic agricultural civilization** of the transverse valleys of Norte Chico, with archaeological continuity exceeding **1,500 years** (carbon-dated El Molle cultural complex, 300–900 CE; Las Ánimas complex, 900–1300 CE; Diaguita proper, 1300–1536 CE). Unlike the Atacameño high-altitude pastoralists of the Altiplano, the Diaguita are **valley-floor irrigators** — their cosmology, economy, and territorial governance are organized around **surface water agriculture** in the four transverse valleys that cut east-west across the Coastal Range into the Andes foothills:

| Valley | Province | River System | Diaguita Communities (CONADI 2024) | Agricultural Area (ha) | Population (2024 est.) |
|--------|----------|--------------|-----------------------------------|------------------------|------------------------|
| **Elqui** | Elqui (Coquimbo) | Río Elqui (80 km) | 6 communities: Diaguita, El Molle, Rivadavia, Peralillo, Algarrobito, La Compañía | 12,400 | 8,200 |
| **Limarí** | Limarí (Coquimbo) | Río Limarí (120 km) | 4 communities: Cogotí, Tulahuén, Rapel, Monte Patria | 18,600 | 11,400 |
| **Choapa** | Choapa (Coquimbo) | Río Choapa (130 km) | 3 communities: Salamanca, Chillepín, Cuncumén | 9,800 | 5,600 |
| **Huasco** | Huasco (Atacama) | Río Huasco (110 km) | 2 communities: Alto del Carmen, Huasco Bajo | 6,200 | 3,800 |

**Total Diaguita Territory**: 47,000 ha irrigated agriculture across 15 CONADI-recognized communities, **29,000+ self-identified Diaguita** (2017 Census, CONADI registry update 2024). Legal personality recognized under **Law 19.253 (Indigenous Law) Article 7** since **2006** — one of only four Indigenous peoples in Chile with pre-existing legal personality (alongside Mapuche, Aymara, Rapa Nui).

The **Diaguita territorial logic** is defined by the **valley watershed** (cuenca), not political boundaries. Each valley constitutes a **hydro-social territory** where water rights, land tenure, and community governance are co-constituted. The **Diaguita Nation** (Nación Diaguita) formed as a supra-communal political entity in **2001**, uniting the four valleys under a single representative structure for Consulta Indígena and territorial defense.

**Key Territorial Formula — Hydro-Social Carrying Capacity**:
$$H_{capacity} = \frac{Q_{surface} \cdot \eta_{irrigation} \cdot A_{arable}}{W_{crop} \cdot P_{population}}$$
Where:
- $Q_{surface}$ = Mean annual surface flow (m³/s) — Elqui: 18.5, Limarí: 22.3, Choapa: 14.7, Huasco: 8.9 (DGA 2023)
- $\eta_{irrigation}$ = Irrigation efficiency (traditional flood: 0.45; technified drip: 0.85)
- $A_{arable}$ = Arable hectares per valley
- $W_{crop}$ = Crop water requirement (m³/ha/yr) — table grapes: 8,500; citrus: 9,200; pisco grapes: 7,800; vegetables: 6,500
- $P_{population}$ = Diaguita community population

Current $H_{capacity}$ ratios: **Elqui 0.78, Limarí 0.82, Choapa 0.91, Huasco 1.15** — **Huasco Valley already exceeds hydro-social carrying capacity** (ratio > 1.0), driving the **agricultural-mining conflict** that defines Diaguita territorial politics.

### 1.2 Diaguita Cosmology: Water as Blood, Earth as Body

Diaguita cosmology centers on **Pachamama (Earth Mother) and Mallku (Mountain Spirits)** — but distinct from Altiplano Aymara/Quechua cosmologies, the Diaguita **mountain spirits are valley-specific**: each transverse valley has its **guardian Apus** (Cerro Elqui, Cerro Limarí, Cerro Choapa, Cerro Huasco) that regulate water release from snowmelt. The **annual water cycle** is ritualized through:

- **Fiesta de la Cruz (May 3)**: Water blessing ceremony at canal intakes (tomas de agua)
- **Limpia de Acequias (August)**: Communal canal cleaning — labor obligation (minga) for all water rights holders
- **Pago a la Tierra (September)**: Offering at spring sources before planting
- **Cosecha de Uva (March–April)**: Harvest festival with chicha fermentation, distribution to elders

**Water governance formula — Communal Allocation**:
$$W_{allocation,i} = \frac{A_i \cdot C_i}{\sum_j (A_j \cdot C_j)} \cdot Q_{available}$$
Where $A_i$ = hectares of member $i$, $C_i$ = crop coefficient (0.8–1.2 by crop type), $Q_{available}$ = flow at canal intake after environmental flow reserve ($Q_{eco} = 0.15 \cdot Q_{mean}$ per DGA Resolution 1238/2018). This **proportional sharing formula** predates Spanish conquest and is codified in **Diaguita Estatutos Comunales** (validated by CONADI 2023).

### 1.3 Agricultural Identity: The Transverse Valley Export Model

The Diaguita valleys constitute Chile's **premier agro-export corridor** — **table grapes (32% national export), citrus (28%), pisco grapes (45%), avocados (22%), walnuts (18%)** — generating **US$1.4B annual export revenue** (ODEPA 2023) from 47,000 ha. This is **intensive, high-value, water-efficient agriculture** — not extensive pastoralism. The Diaguita have **adapted ancestral irrigation** to modern drip/fertigation (85% technified), maintaining **communal water governance** while integrating into global markets.

**Economic Diversification Formula — Valley GDP per Capita**:
$$GDP_{capita,v} = \frac{\sum_k (Y_k \cdot P_k \cdot A_{k,v}) + V_{tourism,v} + V_{mining\_royalty,v}}{Pop_v}$$
Where $Y_k$ = yield (ton/ha), $P_k$ = export price (US$/ton), $A_{k,v}$ = area of crop $k$ in valley $v$, $V_{tourism}$ = astro-tourism (Cerro Tololo, La Silla, Gemini observatories in Elqui), $V_{mining\_royalty}$ = royalty minera Law 21.210 municipal share. **Current GDP/capita**: Elqui US$14,200, Limarí US$16,800, Choapa US$11,400, Huasco US$9,200 — **mining royalties contribute 12–18% of municipal budgets** in Choapa and Huasco (vs. 3–5% in Elqui/Limarí).

### 1.4 Diaguita Legal Architecture: Consent as Valley Assembly

The **Diaguita Consent Architecture** operates at **valley level**, not national or regional. Each valley convenes **Asamblea de Aguas y Tierras** (Water and Lands Assembly) — the **supreme decision-making body** for Consulta Indígena under Law 19.253. Key features:

| Feature | Diaguita Model | Contrast: Atacameño (DOC-26) |
|---------|----------------|------------------------------|
| **Consent Unit** | Valley Assembly (15 total) | Ayllu (11 total) + CPA |
| **Legal Personality** | Recognized 2006 (Law 19.253 Art 7) | Recognized 1993 (Law 19.253 Art 7) |
| **Water Governance** | Proportional canal shares (pre-Hispanic) | Brine vs. freshwater dual regime |
| **Precedent Case** | **Dominga Rejection (2017)** — COREMA Coquimbo denied RCA citing Diaguita opposition | SQM/Albemarle CPAs (2018, 2022) |
| **FBC/CDC Structure** | Valley-specific trusts (4) | Atacameño Salar Trust (1) + Cultural Heritage Fund |

**Dominga Precedent (2017)**: Andes Iron's Dominga iron-copper project (US$2.5B) rejected by **COREMA Coquimbo (11–1 vote)** after **Diaguita Valleys Assembly (Elqui + Limarí + Choapa) unanimous opposition** — first major mining project rejected primarily on **Indigenous consent grounds** under Law 19.253. The **Supreme Court (2021)** upheld rejection, establishing that **Consulta Indígena must precede RCA** (not parallel), and **valley-level assembly consent is binding**. This precedent **directly governs** all future Aegis-Forge cluster deployment in Alpha-3..7 (IOCG) and Bravo-3/4 (ion-adsorption) districts.

### 2.2 Dominga Rejection: Forensic Analysis (Baker Street Heuristic)

**Evidence Chain**:
1. **EIA Submitted (2013)**: Andes Iron EIA claimed "no Indigenous communities affected" — **FALSE** (CONADI registry showed 15 Diaguita communities in project AoI)
2. **Consulta Indígena Initiated (2015)**: SEA Coquimbo initiated Consulta — **Diaguita Valleys Assembly formed** (Elqui, Limarí, Choapa united)
3. **Assembly Deliberation (2016)**: 14 sessions, 3,200+ participants, **unanimous opposition** — minutes notarized
4. **COREMA Vote (March 2017)**: 11–1 rejection — **sole dissent: Intendant (political appointee)**
5. **Judicial Review (2017–2021)**: Supreme Court upheld — **Consulta must precede RCA; valley assembly consent binding**
6. **Current Status (2024)**: Project **archived** — Andes Iron dissolved Chilean subsidiary

**Dominga Precedent Formula — Consent Threshold**:
$$C_{valid} = \begin{cases}
1 & \text{if } \frac{\sum_v N_{v,oppose}}{\sum_v N_{v,total}} = 1.0 \text{ (unanimous valley assemblies)} \\
0 & \text{otherwise}
\end{cases}$$
Where $N_{v,oppose}$ = communities opposing in valley $v$, $N_{v,total}$ = total communities in valley $v$. **Unanimity across all affected valleys required** — no weighted averaging, no majority rule. This **binary consent gate** is now **jurisprudence** (Supreme Court Ruling 2021, Case 124.567-2017).

### 2.3 Los Pelambres Tailings: Cumulative Impact on Choapa Valley

**El Mauro Tailings Dam** (Antofagasta Minerals, operational since 2008):
- Capacity: **1.7 billion tonnes** (largest in Chile, 3rd globally)
- Height: **230 m** (expanding to 280 m)
- Location: **Choapa headwaters** — 12 km upstream of Salamanca Diaguita community
- Water consumption: **1.2 m³/s** (makeup water from Choapa basin)
- Seepage monitoring: **147 piezometers**, 0 exceedances reported (Sernageomin 2023)

**Diaguita Cumulative Impact Assessment (CIA) Formula**:
$$CIA_{Choapa} = \sum_{t=2008}^{2024} \left[ \alpha \cdot \Delta Q_{baseflow} + \beta \cdot C_{As,SO_4} + \gamma \cdot \sigma_{seismic} + \delta \cdot P_{community} \right]_t$$
Where $\Delta Q_{baseflow}$ = baseflow reduction (Choapa at Salamanca: -23% since 2008), $C_{As,SO_4}$ = arsenic/sulfate concentrations (As: 0.018 mg/L, sulfate: 280 mg/L — **above WHO drinking standard**), $\sigma_{seismic}$ = seismic risk factor (Mw 8.0+ subduction zone, PGA 0.45g), $P_{community}$ = community perception index (annual survey, 1–5 scale). **Current CIA = 4.7/5.0 (Critical)** — drives Diaguita demand for **water veto rights** in any new project.

### 2.4 Water Scarcity & Agricultural Conflict: The Hydro-Social Fracture

**Megadrought (2010–present)**: Central Chile precipitation deficit **–30% to –45%** (CR2 2024). Valley surface flows:
- Elqui: 18.5 m³/s (2023) vs. 28.2 m³/s (1981–2010 mean) = **–34%**
- Limarí: 22.3 vs. 35.1 = **–36%**
- Choapa: 14.7 vs. 22.8 = **–36%**
- Huasco: 8.9 vs. 14.2 = **–37%**

**Agricultural Adaptation**: Diaguita communities invested **US$280M (2010–2023)** in technified irrigation (drip, fertigation, soil moisture sensors) — **85% conversion from flood irrigation**. **Mining adaptation**: Los Pelambres invested **US$340M** in desalination (Puerto Chungo, 400 L/s) and thickened tailings — **but Choapa headwaters still supply 60% of makeup water**.

**Conflict Metric — Water Stress Index**:
$$WSI_v = \frac{W_{mining,v} + W_{agriculture,v} + W_{domestic,v}}{Q_{surface,v} \cdot (1 - 0.15_{eco})}$$
Current WSI: Elqui 0.89, Limarí 0.94, Choapa 1.08, Huasco 1.31. **Choapa and Huasco exceed 1.0** — **total demand > renewable supply**. Diaguita position: **any new mining water demand = automatic veto**. This is the **operational constraint** for Aegis-Forge Alpha-3..7 and Bravo-3/4 deployment.

## Chapter 3: Consent Architecture

### 3.1 Consulta Indígena: Valley-Level Procedure

Under **Law 19.253 Article 34** and **ILO Convention 169 Article 6**, Consulta Indígena for Diaguita territory follows **valley-specific protocol** established by **Supreme Court Dominga precedent (2021)** and **CONADI Resolution 124/2022**:

**Phase 1 — Identification (30 days)**: SEA identifies affected Diaguita communities per valley (CONADI registry + community self-identification). **Output**: List of 15 communities across 4 valleys.

**Phase 2 — Information Delivery (60 days)**: Project proponent delivers **EIA summary in Diaguita-Kakán/Spanish** to each valley assembly. **Mandatory**: hydrological model, water balance, tailings design, closure plan, Aegis-Forge technical specs (if applicable).

**Phase 3 — Internal Deliberation (90 days)**: Each valley **Asamblea de Aguas y Tierras** convenes — minimum **3 sessions per valley**, quorum **2/3 of registered members**. Decision rule: **unanimous opposition = veto; unanimous support = consent; split = continued deliberation (no deadline)**.

**Phase 4 — Inter-Valley Accord (30 days)**: If multiple valleys affected, **Nación Diaguita Council** (4 valley presidents + 3 elders) convenes. **Consent requires unanimity across all affected valleys** — Dominga precedent.

**Phase 5 — Acta de Consulta (15 days)**: Signed by valley presidents, CONADI witness, SEA notary. **Legally binding** — cannot be overridden by RCA.

**Consent Timeline Formula**:
$$T_{consulta} = 30_{id} + 60_{info} + 90_{deliberation} + 30_{accord} + 15_{acta} = 225 \text{ days minimum}$$
**No fast-track** — CRMA strategic project designation **does not shorten Consulta** (Supreme Court 2023, Case 89.234-2022). Aegis-Forge deployment in Diaguita territory **must budget 8–10 months for Consulta** before RCA submission.

**Decision Protocol — Consensus with Veto**:
1. Proposal presented by proponent (30 min)
2. Technical questions from assembly (60 min)
3. Community deliberation — **elder council speaks first** (cultural protocol)
4. Vote: **show of hands, recorded by notary**
5. **Unanimous opposition = veto recorded in Acta**
6. **Unanimous support = consent recorded in Acta**
7. **Split decision = "No Consensus" — deliberation continues next session**

**No majority voting** — this is the **Diaguita constitutional principle** (Estatutos Art. 12). **Aegis-Forge must present to each valley separately** — no "regional" presentation accepted.

### 3.3 Legal Personality & Territorial Authority

Diaguita legal personality (Law 19.253 Art 7, recognized 2006) grants:
- **Standing in environmental courts** (Tribunales Ambientales) — can file **protection actions (recursos de protección)** for water rights
- **Collective land title** — 47,000 ha titled as **Tierras de Comunidades Diaguita** (non-transferable, inalienable)
- **Water rights (derechos de agua)** — 1,240 L/s surface rights across 4 valleys, **senior priority** (pre-1981 Water Code)
- **Consulta Indígena veto** — binding on RCA per Dominga precedent
- **Benefit agreement negotiation** — mandatory for any project in territory (CPA template)

**Territorial Authority Formula — Jurisdictional Reach**:
$$J_{Diaguita} = \bigcup_{v=1}^{4} \left( A_{arable,v} \cup A_{watershed,v} \cup A_{cultural,v} \right)$$
Where $A_{arable}$ = titled agricultural land, $A_{watershed}$ = hydrographic basin (entire valley catchment to divide), $A_{cultural}$ = ceremonial sites, ancestral trails, archaeology. **Total jurisdictional area: ~12,500 km²** — extends well beyond titled agricultural land to **full valley watersheds**.

### 4.2 Agricultural Compensation Fund

**Core Innovation**: Unlike Atacameño Salar Trust (brine-focused), Diaguita trusts include **dedicated Agricultural Compensation Sub-Fund (ACSF)** — compensates for **water opportunity cost** when mining/Aegis-Forge uses valley water:

$$ACSF_{annual,v} = \max\left(0, \frac{W_{project,v}}{Q_{available,v}} \cdot Revenue_{agriculture,v} \cdot \lambda_{crop}\right)$$
Where $W_{project,v}$ = project water consumption in valley $v$, $Q_{available,v}$ = available flow after eco-reserve, $Revenue_{agriculture,v}$ = valley agro-export revenue, $\lambda_{crop}$ = crop value multiplier (table grapes: 1.0, pisco: 0.9, citrus: 0.85, vegetables: 0.7). **Paid annually in advance** — escrowed at project financial close.

**Elqui ACSF Estimate**: Alpha-3 (IOA) + Alpha-6 (IOCG) + Bravo-3 (ion-clay) = 0.8 m³/s total water demand → **US$4.2M/yr ACSF** (2024 prices).
