# Chapter 1: NdFeB Grade Specifications — Complete Matrix

Neodymium-Iron-Boron (Nd₂Fe₁₄B) sintered magnets remain the workhorse of the permanent magnet industry. This chapter provides the definitive grade matrix for CSM supply chain procurement, linking each grade to Chilean source minerals (DOC-01), shipping requirements (DOC-08 Ch. 3), and factory intake specs (DOC-08 Ch. 7).


## 1.1 Grade Nomenclature System

**IEC 60404-8-1 / JIS C 2510 / ASTM A977** classification:

```
Grade Format: N XX [H/SH/EH/UH/AH]
├── N = NdFeB
├── XX = (BH)max in MGOe (nominal)
└── Suffix = Intrinsic coercivity (HcJ) tier
    ├── (none) = Standard  ~955 kA/m
    ├── H  = High        ~1,114 kA/m
    ├── SH = Super High  ~1,353 kA/m
    ├── EH = Extra High  ~1,592 kA/m
    ├── UH = Ultra High  ~1,990 kA/m
    └── AH = Absolute High ~2,388 kA/m
```

## 1.4 Magnetic Property Formulas (NdFeB)

**Energy Product Maximum (Theoretical)**:
$$(BH)_{max} = \frac{B_r^2}{4\mu_0} \quad \text{where } \mu_0 = 4\pi \times 10^{-7} \text{ H/m}$$

For N48SH ($B_r = 1.395$ T):
$$(BH)_{max} = \frac{1.395^2}{4 \times 4\pi \times 10^{-7}} = 387 \text{ kJ/m}^3 = 48.7 \text{ MGOe}$$

**Temperature Coefficient of Remanence**:
$$\alpha_{Br} = \frac{1}{B_r} \frac{dB_r}{dT} \approx -0.11\%/°C \text{ (N48SH)}$$

**Irreversible Loss at Temperature $T$**:
$$\frac{\Delta B_r}{B_r} = \exp\left[-\left(\frac{T - T_{ref}}{T_0}\right)^2\right] \quad \text{where } T_0 \text{ depends on } H_{cJ}$$

**Coercivity Temperature Dependence (Kronmüller model)**:
$$H_{cJ}(T) = H_{cJ}(T_0) \left[1 - \alpha_{HcJ} (T - T_0)\right] \quad \alpha_{HcJ} \approx -0.55\%/°C \text{ (N48SH)}$$

**Demagnetization Curve (Jiles-Atherton Model)**:
$$M(H) = M_{an} + M_{rev} = M_s \left[\coth(H_e + \alpha M) - \frac{1}{H_e + \alpha M}\right] + c \frac{dM_{an}}{dH_e}$$
Where $H_e = H + \alpha M$, $M_s$ = saturation magnetization, $\alpha$ = domain wall density parameter, $c$ = domain wall flexibility coefficient.

**Hysteresis Loss per Cycle**:
$$W_h = \oint M dH = \mu_0 \int_{-H_{max}}^{H_{max}} M(H) dH \approx k_h B_{max}^\alpha f$$
With $\alpha \approx 1.6-2.0$ for NdFeB, $k_h \approx 0.02-0.05$ W/kg at 50 Hz, 1.5 T.

**Eddy Current Loss (Laminated vs Solid)**:
$$P_e = \frac{\pi^2 f^2 B_{max}^2 d^2}{6 \rho} \quad \text{(solid)}$$
$$P_e = \frac{\pi^2 f^2 B_{max}^2 t^2}{6 \rho} \quad \text{(laminated, thickness } t)$$
For NdFeB ($\rho \approx 150 \mu\Omega\cdot\text{cm}$), 1mm solid at 1 kHz, 1 T: $P_e \approx 110$ W/kg → **lamination essential for high-freq**.

## 2.4 SmCo Property Formulas

**Energy Product (2:17 Series — Higher $B_r$)**:
$$(BH)_{max} \approx \frac{B_r^2}{4\mu_0} \left(1 - \frac{H_{cB}}{H_{cJ}}\right) \quad \text{(rectangular loop approximation)}$$

For S30H ($B_r = 1.075$ T, $H_{cB}/H_{cJ} \approx 0.45$):
$$(BH)_{max} \approx \frac{1.075^2}{4\mu_0} (1 - 0.45) = 236 \text{ kJ/m}^3 = 29.7 \text{ MGOe}$$

**Temperature Stability Advantage (vs NdFeB)**:
$$\frac{\Delta B_r/B_r}{\Delta T} \bigg|_{\text{SmCo}} \approx \frac{1}{3} \frac{\Delta B_r/B_r}{\Delta T} \bigg|_{\text{NdFeB}}$$

**Curie Temperature ($T_c$)**:
- SmCo 1:5: $T_c \approx 750°C$
- SmCo 2:17: $T_c \approx 820°C$

**No Irreversible Loss up to $T_{max}$** — Unlike NdFeB, SmCo exhibits **fully reversible** temperature behavior within operating range.

**SmCo Demagnetization Curve (Straight-Line Approximation)**:
$$B(H) = B_r + \mu_0 H_{cB} \left(\frac{H}{H_{cB}}\right) \quad \text{for } |H| < H_{cB}$$
**Recoil Permeability**: $\mu_{rec} \approx 1.05-1.15 \mu_0$ (near-unity, minimal self-demagnetization)

**Thermal Demagnetization Kinetics (Néel-Arrhenius)**:
$$\tau = \tau_0 \exp\left(\frac{K V}{k_B T}\right) \quad \text{where } K \text{ = anisotropy constant, } V \text{ = grain volume}$$
For SmCo 2:17: $K \approx 15-20 \text{ MJ/m}^3$, $V \approx 10^{-24} \text{ m}^3$ → $\tau > 10^{10}$ years at 350°C.

# Chapter 3: Fe₁₆N₂ — Alpha-double-prime Iron Nitride

The strategic game-changer: **zero rare earth, zero critical minerals, DFARS-compliant, 27 MGOe target**. Niron Magnetics (US) has demonstrated 27 MGOe at lab scale; Aegis-Forge Chile targets pilot production from Coquimbo Fe-N laterite.


## 3.1 Crystal Structure & Phase Stability

**α"-Fe₁₆N₂ (Alpha-double-prime)**:
- **Structure**: Body-centered cubic (BCC) superlattice, $a = 5.72$ Å, $c = 6.28$ Å
- **Space Group**: $I4/mmm$ (tetragonal distortion of BCC)
- **Nitrogen Sites**: Octahedral interstices, ordered in 16:2 ratio
- **Magnetization**: 2.4 T saturation (theoretical), 1.8-2.0 T achieved
- **Metastable**: Decomposes > 80°C → α-Fe + Fe₄N (γ'-phase)

**Phase Diagram Constraint**:
$$\text{Fe}_{16}\text{N}_2 \xrightarrow{T > 80°C} 16\alpha\text{-Fe} + 2\text{Fe}_4\text{N} \quad \Delta H = -15 \text{ kJ/mol (exothermic)}$$

**Critical Implication**: **Hard temperature limit of 80°C** — not just demagnetization but **phase destruction**. Requires -20°C ± 2°C shipping (DOC-08 Ch. 3).

## 3.4 Fe₁₆N₂ Property Formulas

**Saturation Magnetization (Theoretical)**:
$$M_s = \frac{16 \times 2.22 + 2 \times 0}{16 \times 55.85 + 2 \times 14.01} \times N_A \mu_B = 2.4 \text{ T} \quad \text{(where 2.22 } \mu_B/\text{Fe)}$$

**Nitrogen Content Control**:
$$\text{N at\%} = \frac{2}{18} \times 100 = 11.1\% \quad \text{Target: } 10.8-11.5\%$$

**Decomposition Kinetics (Arrhenius)**:
$$k = A \exp\left(-\frac{E_a}{RT}\right) \quad E_a \approx 120 \text{ kJ/mol}$$
At 80°C (353 K): $t_{1/2} \approx 100$ hrs → **shipping must maintain < 60°C**

**Energy Product Scaling with N Content**:
$$(BH)_{max} \propto (x_N - x_{crit})^2 \quad x_{crit} \approx 9.5\%$$

**Nitridation Reaction Kinetics (Gas-Solid)**:
$$\frac{d\alpha}{dt} = k (1-\alpha)^n \quad \text{where } \alpha = \text{fraction nitrided}$$
$$k = k_0 \exp\left(-\frac{E_{nit}}{RT}\right) P_{N_2}^m$$
Typical: $T = 450-550°C$, $P_{N_2} = 0.5-1.0$ atm, $t = 10-50$ hrs, $E_{nit} \approx 80-100 \text{ kJ/mol}$.

**Magnetic Anisotropy Origin (Crystal Field Theory)**:
$$K_1 = \frac{3}{2} \lambda \langle r^2 \rangle \alpha_J \quad \text{(single-ion)}$$
For Fe₁₆N₂: $K_1 \approx 1.5-2.0 \text{ MJ/m}^3$ (lower than NdFeB's 4.5 MJ/m³) → limits $H_{cJ}$.

**Thermal Conductivity Impact on Nitridation Uniformity**:
$$\nabla^2 T = \frac{1}{\alpha_{th}} \frac{\partial T}{\partial t} + \frac{Q_{rxn}}{k_{th}} \quad \alpha_{th} \approx 20 \text{ mm}^2/\text{s (Fe)}$$
Exothermic nitridation ($\Delta H \approx -50 \text{ kJ/mol}$) → thermal gradients → N non-uniformity → **controlled ramp rates essential**.

# Chapter 4: Fe-Co — High-Flux Soft Magnetic Alloys

## 4.3 Fe-Co Property Formulas

**Saturation Flux Density (Slater-Pauling Curve)**:
$$B_s = \mu_0 M_s = \mu_0 \frac{N_A}{V_m} \mu_B (n_{Fe} \cdot 2.22 + n_{Co} \cdot 1.72)$$

For Fe₅₀Co₅₀:
$$B_s = 4\pi \times 10^{-7} \times \frac{6.022 \times 10^{23}}{7.1 \times 10^{-6}} \times 9.27 \times 10^{-24} \times (0.5 \times 2.22 + 0.5 \times 1.72) = 2.42 \text{ T}$$

**Core Loss (Steinmetz Equation)**:
$$P_{core} = k_h f B^\alpha + k_e f^2 B^2 + k_a f^{1.5} B^{1.5}$$
- Hysteresis: $k_h \approx 0.02$ W/kg (at 50 Hz, 1.5 T)
- Eddy current: $k_e \approx 0.0005$ (lamination dependent)
- Anomalous: $k_a \approx 0.001$

**Permeability vs. Flux Density**:
$$\mu(B) = \frac{B}{H} \approx \mu_{max} \exp\left[-\left(\frac{B}{B_{knee}}\right)^2\right] \quad B_{knee} \approx 1.8 \text{ T}$$

**Magnetostriction (Villari Effect — Stress Induced Anisotropy)**:
$$\lambda(\sigma) = \lambda_s \left[1 - \exp\left(-\frac{\sigma}{\sigma_0}\right)\right] \quad \lambda_s \approx 60-80 \text{ ppm}$$
For Fe₅₀Co₅₀: $\sigma_0 \approx 100 \text{ MPa}$ → **stress annealing critical** for low $H_c$.

**Electrical Resistivity (Alloy Scattering)**:
$$\rho = \rho_0 + \rho_{mag} + \rho_{imp}$$
Fe₅₀Co₅₀: $\rho \approx 25-30 \mu\Omega\cdot\text{cm}$ (vs Si-Fe: 50 $\mu\Omega\cdot\text{cm}$) → **higher eddy losses, thinner lams needed**.

**High-Frequency Permeability (Snoek's Limit)**:
$$\mu_{max} f_r = \frac{\gamma}{2\pi} M_s \approx 1.5 \times 10^{10} \text{ Hz} \quad \text{(for Fe-Co)}$$
Where $\gamma = 1.76 \times 10^{11} \text{ rad/s·T}$, $M_s = 1.92 \times 10^6 \text{ A/m}$.
