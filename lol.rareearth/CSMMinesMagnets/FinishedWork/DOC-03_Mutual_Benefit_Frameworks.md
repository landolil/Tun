## Chilean Royalty Regime Analysis -- Law 21,210
## 1. Chilean Royalty Regime Analysis -- Law 21,210 and Municipal Fiscal Architecture

Chile's mineral royalty regime underwent fundamental restructuring with Law 21,210 (Royalty Minera), enacted September 2023 and effective January 2024. This chapter provides the mathematical and legal foundation for Tardigradia's community value-return calculations, establishing the fiscal baseline against which all additional benefit mechanisms must be measured.

### 1.1 Legal Framework and Legislative History

| Element | Detail |
|---------|--------|
| Law Number | Law 21,210 (Royalty Minera) |
| Publication Date | September 28, 2023 (Diario Oficial) |
| Effective Date | January 1, 2024 |
| Repealed Law | Law 18,248 (1984) specific royalty provisions |
| Constitutional Basis | Article 19, Section 21 (property rights) + Transitory Provision 17 |

The legislative process (2019-2023) reflected tension between fiscal need (pandemic recovery, social demands) and industry competitiveness. Final text balances ad valorem simplicity with margin-based progressivity -- a hybrid design unique in Latin America.

### 1.2 Dual-Rate Structure -- Mathematical Specification

#### 1.2.1 Copper Producers (>50 kt/yr fine copper)

The royalty comprises two simultaneous components:

**Component A -- Ad Valorem (Gross Sales):**
$$R_{av} = 0.01 \times P_{Cu} \times Q_{Cu}$$

Where $P_{Cu}$ = realized copper price (US$/lb), $Q_{Cu}$ = fine copper production (lbs). This component applies to ALL copper producers regardless of scale.

**Component B -- Operating Margin (Progressive):**
$$R_{om} = \tau(M) \times M$$
Where $M$ = operating margin = $(Revenue - Operating Costs - Depreciation) / Revenue$

Progressive rate schedule $\tau(M)$:

| Operating Margin Range | Marginal Rate $\tau$ | Cumulative Rate at Upper Bound |
|------------------------|---------------------|----------------------------------|
| 0% - 15% | 0% | 0% |
| 15% - 25% | 8% | 0.8% |
| 25% - 35% | 12% | 2.0% |
| 35% - 45% | 18% | 3.8% |
| 45% - 55% | 22% | 6.0% |
| 55% - 65% | 26% | 8.6% |
| >65% | 26% | 8.6%+ |

Effective total royalty rate: $R_{total} = R_{av} + R_{om}$ (as % of revenue).

#### 1.2.2 Lithium Producers

$$R_{Li} = 0.018 \times P_{Li} \times Q_{Li} + \tau_{Li}(M) \times M$$

Lithium margin rates: 6.8% (15-25%), 14% (25-35%), 22% (35-45%), 30% (45-55%), 40% (>55%). Higher rates reflect lithium's strategic criticality and lower processing complexity.

#### 1.2.3 Iron Ore and REE Producers

Iron ore (magnetite/hematite): 1% ad valorem only (no margin component). REE concentrates: 1% ad valorem + 8% margin above 15% threshold. These rates directly affect CAP (iron) and future Aegis-Forge REE separation output.

### 1.3 Distribution Formula -- Municipal and Regional Allocation

Total national collection flows to the **Fondo de Compensación Minera** (Mining Compensation Fund) administered by Subsecretaría de Desarrollo Regional (Subdere):

$$F_{municipal} = 0.65 \times F_{total} \times w_{commune}$$
$$F_{regional} = 0.35 \times F_{total} \times w_{region}$$

Communal weight $w_{commune}$:
$$w_{commune} = 0.50 \times \frac{Pop_{commune}}{\sum Pop} + 0.30 \times \frac{Poverty_{commune}}{\sum Poverty} + 0.20 \times \frac{MiningIntensity_{commune}}{\sum MiningIntensity}$$

Where Mining Intensity = (mining employment + royalty base) / population. This formula creates perverse incentives: municipalities that attract MORE mining get HIGHER weight, reinforcing extraction dependency.

## Community Agreement Models -- Comparative Analysis
## 2. Community Agreement Models -- Comparative Analysis of Chilean Mining Convenios

Chilean mining companies have developed five major community agreement (convenio) templates over two decades. This chapter dissects each model, quantifies value flows, and extracts design principles for Tardigradia's mutual benefit framework. The goal is not replication but synthesis -- combining the strongest elements into a new agreement architecture suited for REE processing and magnet manufacturing.

### 2.1 The Five Reference Agreements -- Deep Dive

#### 2.1.1 Codelco-Andacollo (2008, renewed 2018) -- The Municipal Partnership Model

| Element | Detail |
|---------|--------|
| Parties | Codelco Chile, Municipality of Andacollo, Diaguita Communities |
| Annual Value | $12M (2023) / $14M (2024 adjusted) |
| Duration | 10 years with 5-year review |
| Governance | Tripartite committee: Codelco, Municipality, Community reps (3 each) |

Value distribution (2024):
- Local hiring quota: 40% of operational workforce (currently 38% achieved)
- Scholarship fund: 120 university/technical scholarships/year ($1.2M)
- Health: Codelco funds 60% of Andacollo hospital operating budget ($3.5M)
- Water: $2M/yr for aquifer monitoring, rural potable water, irrigation efficiency
- Productive development: $1.5M for agriculture, tourism, artisan cooperatives
- Community infrastructure: $2M for roads, community centers, sports facilities
- Environmental monitoring: Independent community lab ($800k/yr)

Innovation: Municipal co-investment -- Andacollo matches Codelco 1:3 on productive projects. Municipality becomes equity partner in outcomes, not just recipient.

## Value-Return Mechanisms -- Equity, Technology Transfer, Local Processing
## 3. Value-Return Mechanisms -- Equity Participation, Technology Transfer, Local Processing

Beyond traditional community agreements, Tardigradia deploys three structural value-return mechanisms that fundamentally shift the community from stakeholder to co-owner. These mechanisms are designed to be legally enforceable, financially material, and scalable across multiple Aegis-Forge clusters.

### 3.1 Equity Participation -- Community Ownership in REE Processing

#### 3.1.1 Legal Structure -- Chilean SpA with Community Trust

Tardigradia Chile SpA (Sociedad por Acciones) holds the Aegis-Forge operating entities. A parallel **Community Benefit Trust (Fideicomiso de Beneficio Comunitario)** holds equity on behalf of host communities:

| Entity | Structure | Community Equity | Governance |
|--------|-----------|------------------|------------|
| Tardigradia Chile SpA | Parent holding | 15% (Class B shares) | Trust appoints 2 of 7 directors |
| Aegis-Forge Norte SpA | Cluster 1 (Vallenar/Tierra Amarilla) | 20% | Trust appoints 1 of 5 directors |
| Aegis-Forge Centro SpA | Cluster 2 (Andacollo/Los Andes) | 20% | Trust appoints 1 of 5 directors |
| Aegis-Forge Sur SpA | Cluster 3 (future Austral) | 20% | Trust appoints 1 of 5 directors |

Class B shares carry: (1) Dividend preference (first 8% of distributable cash), (2) Board appointment rights, (3) Veto on community-impacting decisions (water, emissions, closure), (4) Tag-along rights on any control transaction.

#### 3.1.2 Equity Valuation and Vesting

Equity granted at formation (pre-revenue) -- no cash contribution required from communities. Vesting schedule:

- Year 1-2: 25% vested (construction/employment phase)
- Year 3-5: 50% vested (ramp-up, first revenue)
- Year 6-10: 100% vested (steady state)

Anti-dilution protection: Community equity participates pro-rata in all future capital raises. If Tardigradia raises at >$500M valuation, community trust receives additional shares to maintain percentage.

#### 3.1.3 Dividend Flow Calculation -- 10-Unit Cluster Example

Assumptions per Aegis-Forge unit (500 kg/cycle, 18,250 kg/yr REE oxide at 99.5%):

- Revenue/unit: $821,250/yr (basket $45/kg)
- OPEX/unit: $4.32/kg × 18,250 = $78,840/yr (E172 spec)
- CAPEX amortization (10 yr): $1.84M/yr
- EBITDA/unit: $821,250 - $78,840 - $184,000 (G&A) = $558,410
- 10-unit cluster EBITDA: $5.58M/yr
- Community trust share (15% parent + 20% opco): ~$1.1M/yr distributable
- Per capita (pop 12,000 across 3 communes): ~$92/person/yr from dividends alone

Combined with $3,000/person/yr direct investment: **Total ~$3,092/person/yr** -- 48% above Candelaria benchmark.

### 8.5 Exit Alignment -- Protecting Community Value

Scenario analysis for change of control (Tardigradia acquired, IPO, asset sale):

| Exit Type | Community Protection | Mechanism |
|-----------|---------------------|-----------|
| Trade Sale (100%) | Tag-along: FBC sells pro-rata at same price/terms | Shareholders Agreement Art 12 |
| Partial Sale (control) | FBC approval required (2/3 community directors) | Shareholders Agreement Art 8 |
| IPO | FBC retains shares, lock-up 18 months, registration rights | Shareholders Agreement Art 15 |
| Asset Sale (opco) | FBC opco tag-along + CDC first refusal on community assets | Opco Shareholders Agreement |
| Distress/Insolvency | Environmental bonds first claim; CDC assets ring-fenced | Trust deed + bond indenture |

Key principle: **Community equity is not diluted, not forced to sell below fair value, and retains governance rights through any transition.**
