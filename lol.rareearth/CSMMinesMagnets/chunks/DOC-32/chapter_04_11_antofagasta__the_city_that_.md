### 1.1.2 Urban Form — Five Sectors, One Coast

| Sector | Population | Character | Economic Function | Key Challenges |
|--------|------------|-----------|-------------------|----------------|
| **Centro** | 85,000 | Historic, commercial, administrative | Port services, government, retail, tourism | Congestion, heritage preservation, aging infrastructure |
| **Norte** | 110,000 | Residential middle/upper, universities | Education (UA, UCN), health, services | Water cost, transport, green space deficit |
| **Sur** | 95,000 | Industrial, logistics, port-adjacent | **EMPORCHA, ATI, rail yards, fuel terminals** | Pollution, heavy traffic, tsunami risk, informal housing |
| **La Chimba** | 72,000 | **Largest campamento sector**, migrant-dense | Informal commerce, construction labor | **42 campamentos**, land tenure, services, integration |
| **Coloso** | 50,000 | New development, gated communities | Mining executive housing, country clubs | Segregation, water demand, car dependence |

**Coastal Interface**: **30 km coastline** — Mejillones Peninsula (north) to Caleta Coloso (south). **Camanchaca (coastal fog) zone** — historic water source, now declining.

### 1.1.3 Historical Trajectory — Boom/Bust Cycles

| Era | Period | Driver | Population | Legacy |
|-----|--------|--------|------------|--------|
| **Pre-Colonial** | <1536 | Chango fishing/gathering | ~2,000 | Coastal territory, camanchaca harvesting, shellfish |
| **Bolivian Port** | 1825–1879 | Silver/copper export (Huanchaca) | 5,000 | Plaza Colón, Customs House, railway to Salar |
| **Nitrate Boom** | 1879–1930 | **Chilean annexation, nitrate monopoly** | 5,000 → **90,000** | Victorian architecture, FCAB railway, British/Chilean elite, labor movement origins |
| **Nitrate Bust** | 1930–1950 | Synthetic nitrate (Haber-Bosch) | Stagnant 90k | Unemployment, migration south, union radicalization |
| **Copper Era** | 1950–1990 | **Chuquicamata, Escondida, Spence** | 90k → **230,000** | Port modernization, Escondida (1990), ZOFRI (1994), service economy |
| **Lithium/REE Transition** | 1990–2024 | **Salar de Atacama (SQM, Albemarle), REE exploration** | 230k → **412,000** | Desalination 100%, solar corridor, HIF H₂, Aegis-Forge export gateway |
| **Future: Green Export Hub** | 2025–2035 | **Green H₂/NH₃, REE magnets, data centers, sovereign cloud** | Projected **500,000** | Decarbonized port, circular economy, sovereign digital infrastructure |

**Key Insight**: Antofagasta has **reinvented itself 4×** in 150 years — each cycle extracts more value, employs more people, but **deepens inequality and ecological footprint**. The REE/Green H₂ transition must break this pattern.
