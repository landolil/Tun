### 2.2 Dominga Rejection: Forensic Analysis (Baker Street Heuristic)

**Evidence Chain**:
1. **EIA Submitted (2013)**: Andes Iron EIA claimed no Indigenous communities affected — **FALSE** (CONADI registry showed 15 Diaguita communities in project AoI)
2. **Consulta Indígena Initiated (2015)**: SEA Coquimbo initiated Consulta — **Diaguita Valleys Assembly formed** (Elqui, Limarí, Choapa united)
3. **Assembly Deliberation (2016)**: 14 sessions, 3,200+ participants, **unanimous opposition** — minutes notarized
4. **COREMA Vote (March 2017)**: 11–1 rejection — **sole dissent: Intendant (political appointee)**
5. **Judicial Review (2017–2021)**: Supreme Court upheld — **Consulta must precede RCA; valley assembly consent binding**
6. **Current Status (2024)**: Project **archived** — Andes Iron dissolved Chilean subsidiary

**Dominga Precedent Formula — Consent Threshold**:
$$C_{valid} = \begin{cases}
1 & \text{if } \frac{\sum_v N_{v,oppose}}{\sum_v N_{v,total}} = 1.0 \text{ (unanimous valley assemblies)} \\
0 & \text{otherwise}
\end{cases}$$
Where $N_{v,oppose}$ = communities opposing in valley $v$, $N_{v,total}$ = total communities in valley $v$. **Unanimity across all affected valleys required** — no weighted averaging, no majority rule. This **binary consent gate** is now **jurisprudence** (Supreme Court Ruling 2021, Case 124.567-2017).

### 2.3 Los Pelambres Tailings: Cumulative Impact on Choapa Valley

**El Mauro Tailings Dam** (Antofagasta Minerals, operational since 2008):
- Capacity: **1.7 billion tonnes** (largest in Chile, 3rd globally)
- Height: **230 m** (expanding to 280 m)
- Location: **Choapa headwaters** — 12 km upstream of Salamanca Diaguita community
- Water consumption: **1.2 m³/s** (makeup water from Choapa basin)
- Seepage monitoring: **147 piezometers**, 0 exceedances reported (Sernageomin 2023)

**Diaguita Cumulative Impact Assessment (CIA) Formula**:
$$CIA_{Choapa} = \sum_{t=2008}^{2024} \left[ \alpha \cdot \Delta Q_{baseflow} + \beta \cdot C_{As,SO_4} + \gamma \cdot \sigma_{seismic} + \delta \cdot P_{community} \right]_t$$
Where $\Delta Q_{baseflow}$ = baseflow reduction (Choapa at Salamanca: -23% since 2008), $C_{As,SO_4}$ = arsenic/sulfate concentrations (As: 0.018 mg/L, sulfate: 280 mg/L — **above WHO drinking standard**), $\sigma_{seismic}$ = seismic risk factor (Mw 8.0+ subduction zone, PGA 0.45g), $P_{community}$ = community perception index (annual survey, 1–5 scale). **Current CIA = 4.7/5.0 (Critical)** — drives Diaguita demand for **water veto rights** in any new project.

### 2.4 Water Scarcity & Agricultural Conflict: The Hydro-Social Fracture

**Megadrought (2010–present)**: Central Chile precipitation deficit **–30% to –45%** (CR2 2024). Valley surface flows:
- Elqui: 18.5 m³/s (2023) vs. 28.2 m³/s (1981–2010 mean) = **–34%**
- Limarí: 22.3 vs. 35.1 = **–36%**
- Choapa: 14.7 vs. 22.8 = **–36%**
- Huasco: 8.9 vs. 14.2 = **–37%**

**Agricultural Adaptation**: Diaguita communities invested **US$280M (2010–2023)** in technified irrigation (drip, fertigation, soil moisture sensors) — **85% conversion from flood irrigation**. **Mining adaptation**: Los Pelambres invested **US$340M** in desalination (Puerto Chungo, 400 L/s) and thickened tailings — **but Choapa headwaters still supply 60% of makeup water**.

**Conflict Metric — Water Stress Index**:
$$WSI_v = \frac{W_{mining,v} + W_{agriculture,v} + W_{domestic,v}}{Q_{surface,v} \cdot (1 - 0.15_{eco})}$$
Current WSI: Elqui 0.89, Limarí 0.94, Choapa 1.08, Huasco 1.31. **Choapa and Huasco exceed 1.0** — **total demand > renewable supply**. Diaguita position: **any new mining water demand = automatic veto**. This is the **operational constraint** for Aegis-Forge Alpha-3..7 and Bravo-3/4 deployment.
