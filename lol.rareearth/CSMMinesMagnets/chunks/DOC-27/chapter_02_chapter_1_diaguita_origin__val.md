## Chapter 1: Diaguita Origin & Valleys

### 1.1 Diaguita Origin and Territorial Identity

The Diaguita people (Diaguita-Kakán linguistic family) are the **pre-Inca, pre-Hispanic agricultural civilization** of the transverse valleys of Norte Chico, with archaeological continuity exceeding **1,500 years** (carbon-dated El Molle cultural complex, 300–900 CE; Las Ánimas complex, 900–1300 CE; Diaguita proper, 1300–1536 CE). Unlike the Atacameño high-altitude pastoralists of the Altiplano, the Diaguita are **valley-floor irrigators** — their cosmology, economy, and territorial governance are organized around **surface water agriculture** in the four transverse valleys that cut east-west across the Coastal Range into the Andes foothills:

| Valley | Province | River System | Diaguita Communities (CONADI 2024) | Agricultural Area (ha) | Population (2024 est.) |
|--------|----------|--------------|-----------------------------------|------------------------|------------------------|
| **Elqui** | Elqui (Coquimbo) | Río Elqui (80 km) | 6 communities: Diaguita, El Molle, Rivadavia, Peralillo, Algarrobito, La Compañía | 12,400 | 8,200 |
| **Limarí** | Limarí (Coquimbo) | Río Limarí (120 km) | 4 communities: Cogotí, Tulahuén, Rapel, Monte Patria | 18,600 | 11,400 |
| **Choapa** | Choapa (Coquimbo) | Río Choapa (130 km) | 3 communities: Salamanca, Chillepín, Cuncumén | 9,800 | 5,600 |
| **Huasco** | Huasco (Atacama) | Río Huasco (110 km) | 2 communities: Alto del Carmen, Huasco Bajo | 6,200 | 3,800 |

**Total Diaguita Territory**: 47,000 ha irrigated agriculture across 15 CONADI-recognized communities, **29,000+ self-identified Diaguita** (2017 Census, CONADI registry update 2024). Legal personality recognized under **Law 19.253 (Indigenous Law) Article 7** since **2006** — one of only four Indigenous peoples in Chile with pre-existing legal personality (alongside Mapuche, Aymara, Rapa Nui).

The **Diaguita territorial logic** is defined by the **valley watershed** (cuenca), not political boundaries. Each valley constitutes a **hydro-social territory** where water rights, land tenure, and community governance are co-constituted. The **Diaguita Nation** (Nación Diaguita) formed as a supra-communal political entity in **2001**, uniting the four valleys under a single representative structure for Consulta Indígena and territorial defense.

**Key Territorial Formula — Hydro-Social Carrying Capacity**:
$$H_{capacity} = \frac{Q_{surface} \cdot \eta_{irrigation} \cdot A_{arable}}{W_{crop} \cdot P_{population}}$$
Where:
- $Q_{surface}$ = Mean annual surface flow (m³/s) — Elqui: 18.5, Limarí: 22.3, Choapa: 14.7, Huasco: 8.9 (DGA 2023)
- $\eta_{irrigation}$ = Irrigation efficiency (traditional flood: 0.45; technified drip: 0.85)
- $A_{arable}$ = Arable hectares per valley
- $W_{crop}$ = Crop water requirement (m³/ha/yr) — table grapes: 8,500; citrus: 9,200; pisco grapes: 7,800; vegetables: 6,500
- $P_{population}$ = Diaguita community population

Current $H_{capacity}$ ratios: **Elqui 0.78, Limarí 0.82, Choapa 0.91, Huasco 1.15** — **Huasco Valley already exceeds hydro-social carrying capacity** (ratio > 1.0), driving the **agricultural-mining conflict** that defines Diaguita territorial politics.

### 1.2 Diaguita Cosmology: Water as Blood, Earth as Body

Diaguita cosmology centers on **Pachamama (Earth Mother) and Mallku (Mountain Spirits)** — but distinct from Altiplano Aymara/Quechua cosmologies, the Diaguita **mountain spirits are valley-specific**: each transverse valley has its **guardian Apus** (Cerro Elqui, Cerro Limarí, Cerro Choapa, Cerro Huasco) that regulate water release from snowmelt. The **annual water cycle** is ritualized through:

- **Fiesta de la Cruz (May 3)**: Water blessing ceremony at canal intakes (tomas de agua)
- **Limpia de Acequias (August)**: Communal canal cleaning — labor obligation (minga) for all water rights holders
- **Pago a la Tierra (September)**: Offering at spring sources before planting
- **Cosecha de Uva (March–April)**: Harvest festival with chicha fermentation, distribution to elders

**Water governance formula — Communal Allocation**:
$$W_{allocation,i} = \frac{A_i \cdot C_i}{\sum_j (A_j \cdot C_j)} \cdot Q_{available}$$
Where $A_i$ = hectares of member $i$, $C_i$ = crop coefficient (0.8–1.2 by crop type), $Q_{available}$ = flow at canal intake after environmental flow reserve ($Q_{eco} = 0.15 \cdot Q_{mean}$ per DGA Resolution 1238/2018). This **proportional sharing formula** predates Spanish conquest and is codified in **Diaguita Estatutos Comunales** (validated by CONADI 2023).

### 1.3 Agricultural Identity: The Transverse Valley Export Model

The Diaguita valleys constitute Chile's **premier agro-export corridor** — **table grapes (32% national export), citrus (28%), pisco grapes (45%), avocados (22%), walnuts (18%)** — generating **US$1.4B annual export revenue** (ODEPA 2023) from 47,000 ha. This is **intensive, high-value, water-efficient agriculture** — not extensive pastoralism. The Diaguita have **adapted ancestral irrigation** to modern drip/fertigation (85% technified), maintaining **communal water governance** while integrating into global markets.

**Economic Diversification Formula — Valley GDP per Capita**:
$$GDP_{capita,v} = \frac{\sum_k (Y_k \cdot P_k \cdot A_{k,v}) + V_{tourism,v} + V_{mining\_royalty,v}}{Pop_v}$$
Where $Y_k$ = yield (ton/ha), $P_k$ = export price (US$/ton), $A_{k,v}$ = area of crop $k$ in valley $v$, $V_{tourism}$ = astro-tourism (Cerro Tololo, La Silla, Gemini observatories in Elqui), $V_{mining\_royalty}$ = royalty minera Law 21.210 municipal share. **Current GDP/capita**: Elqui US$14,200, Limarí US$16,800, Choapa US$11,400, Huasco US$9,200 — **mining royalties contribute 12–18% of municipal budgets** in Choapa and Huasco (vs. 3–5% in Elqui/Limarí).

### 1.4 Diaguita Legal Architecture: Consent as Valley Assembly

The **Diaguita Consent Architecture** operates at **valley level**, not national or regional. Each valley convenes **Asamblea de Aguas y Tierras** (Water and Lands Assembly) — the **supreme decision-making body** for Consulta Indígena under Law 19.253. Key features:

| Feature | Diaguita Model | Contrast: Atacameño (DOC-26) |
|---------|----------------|------------------------------|
| **Consent Unit** | Valley Assembly (15 total) | Ayllu (11 total) + CPA |
| **Legal Personality** | Recognized 2006 (Law 19.253 Art 7) | Recognized 1993 (Law 19.253 Art 7) |
| **Water Governance** | Proportional canal shares (pre-Hispanic) | Brine vs. freshwater dual regime |
| **Precedent Case** | **Dominga Rejection (2017)** — COREMA Coquimbo denied RCA citing Diaguita opposition | SQM/Albemarle CPAs (2018, 2022) |
| **FBC/CDC Structure** | Valley-specific trusts (4) | Atacameño Salar Trust (1) + Cultural Heritage Fund |

**Dominga Precedent (2017)**: Andes Iron's Dominga iron-copper project (US$2.5B) rejected by **COREMA Coquimbo (11–1 vote)** after **Diaguita Valleys Assembly (Elqui + Limarí + Choapa) unanimous opposition** — first major mining project rejected primarily on **Indigenous consent grounds** under Law 19.253. The **Supreme Court (2021)** upheld rejection, establishing that **Consulta Indígena must precede RCA** (not parallel), and **valley-level assembly consent is binding**. This precedent **directly governs** all future Aegis-Forge cluster deployment in Alpha-3..7 (IOCG) and Bravo-3/4 (ion-adsorption) districts.
