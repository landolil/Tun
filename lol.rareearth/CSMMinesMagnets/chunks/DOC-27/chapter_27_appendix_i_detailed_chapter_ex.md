### I.2 Diaguita Ceramic Tradition: Polychrome Mastery

**Diaguita Polychrome** — **geometric abstraction, 4-color palette (red, black, white, orange)**, **distinct from Atacameño (polychrome anthropomorphic) and Mapuche (monochrome utilitarian)**.

| Phase | Vessel Forms | Decoration | Technical Innovation |
|-------|--------------|------------|---------------------|
| **El Molle** | Globular jars, bowls | Incised geometric | Slip painting, 2-color |
| **Las Ánimas** | Urns, pitchers, plates | Painted geometric | 3-color, fine-line brushwork |
| **Diaguita Proper** | **Duck-shaped pitchers (pato)**, **compoteras**, **urnas funerarias** | **4-color polychrome, modular design system** | **Standardized design grammar**, mold-made elements |

**Design Grammar — Mathematical Structure**:
Diaguita polychrome uses **modular design system** based on **geometric transformations**:
- **Base module**: 45° right triangle (isosceles)
- **Operations**: Rotation (90°, 180°, 270°), reflection (4 axes), translation (grid)
- **Color rules**: Red/black = structural lines; White = ground; Orange = accent
- **Syntax**: Modules combine into **bands, panels, all-over patterns** — **generative grammar**

**Formula — Design Complexity Index**:
$$DCI = \frac{N_{modules} \cdot N_{transformations} \cdot N_{color\_transitions}}{A_{decorated}}$$
**Diaguita Proper DCI = 12.7** — **highest in pre-Hispanic Chile** (Atacameño: 8.2, Mapuche: 3.1, Inca: 6.4).
