## Chapter 3: Consent Architecture — Consulta Indígena, CPA Models, FPIC Standards, Atacameño Legal Personality

### 3.1 Consulta Indígena (Law 19.253) — Procedural Reality

#### 3.1.1 Consulta Process & Atacameño Experience

```
Consulta Indígena Process (SEA / CONADI):

Phase 1: Project Entry (SEA)
    ├── Project enters SEIA (EIA or DIA)
    ├── SEA identifies affected Indigenous peoples (CONADI registry)
    └── SEA issues "Inicio de Consulta" resolution

Phase 2: Information Delivery (Proponent → Communities)
    ├── Proponent provides project info (Spanish + Kunza translation)
    ├── 30-day review period
    └── Community requests for additional info (unlimited)

Phase 3: Dialogue & Consultation (CONADI-facilitated)
    ├── Minimum 3 community assemblies per affected group
    ├── Technical workshops (independent experts)
    ├── Cultural protocol adherence (ceremonial opening/closing)
    └── 60-120 day consultation period

Phase 4: Agreement or Non-Agreement
    ├── Agreement: "Acta de Acuerdo" → signed by community authorities + proponent
    ├── Non-Agreement: "Acta de Desacuerdo" → SEA decides (can approve anyway)
    └── SEA Resolution: RCA granted/denied with conditions

Atacameño Experience (2010-2024):
- **Consultas Completed**: 12 (7 EIA, 5 DIA)
- **Agreements Reached**: 9 (75%)
- **Non-Agreements**: 3 (25%) — SEA approved 2/3 despite desacuerdo
- **Average Duration**: 8.5 months (range: 4-18 months)
- **Key Deficiency**: **Consulta ≠ Consent** — SEA can override desacuerdo
- **Atacameño Response**: **CPA (private contract) as binding consent mechanism**
```

#### 3.1.2 Consulta Limitations & Atacameño Workaround

| Limitation | Impact | Atacameño Workaround |
|------------|--------|---------------------|
| **Non-binding** (SEA can approve despite desacuerdo) | Consent illusion | **CPA = binding private contract** |
| **Proponent controls info** | Asymmetric knowledge | **Independent technical advisors (CPA-funded)** |
| **No veto power** | Communities cannot stop | **CPA Art. 12 = binding veto via hydrology committee** |
| **No benefit sharing mandate** | Voluntary only | **CPA: 20% FBC equity + 15% CDC FCF** |
| **No post-approval enforcement** | Conditions often unmet | **CPA: Independent monitor + environmental bond** |
| **Urban diaspora excluded** | 60% Atacameño in cities | **CPA: Urban ayllu representation in CDA** |

### 3.2 CPA Models — Precedent Analysis

#### 3.2.1 Escondida (BHP) CPA — Gold Standard

```
Escondida CPA Evolution:

CPA 2006 (Original):
- Term: 20 years (2006-2026)
- Community Investment: $25M/yr (escalating 3%/yr)
- Employment: 35% local, training fund $2M/yr
- Water: Desalination commitment (2006-2015 transition)
- Governance: Joint Committee (BHP + Atacameño reps)
- Total 2006-2026: ~$650M community investment

CPA 2016 (Renewal — Current):
- Term: 20 years (2016-2036)
- Community Investment: $60M/yr (escalating 3%/yr)
- Employment: 40% local, training fund $5M/yr
- Water: 100% desalination (achieved 2017)
- Equity: None (BHP resisted)
- Governance: Enhanced — Independent monitor, veto on water
- Total 2016-2036 (projected): ~$1.8B community investment

CPA 2026 (Negotiating):
- Atacameño demand: **Equity stake (5-10%) + Water veto + REE value-add**
- BHP position: Increased investment, no equity
- **Aegis-Forge Benchmark**: **20% FBC equity + 15% CDC FCF + Zero puri veto**
```

#### 3.2.2 Centinela (Antofagasta Minerals) CPA

```
Centinela CPA 2022:
- Term: 20 years
- Community Investment: $40M/yr
- Employment: 38% local, training $3M/yr
- Water: 100% desalination
- Equity: **3% net smelter return (NSR) to community trust** (innovation)
- Governance: Community veto on water + tailings
- Cultural: Kunza language school, ceremonial site protection
- **Aegis-Forge Benchmark**: **20% equity > 3% NSR (control + upside)**
```

#### 3.2.3 Sierra Gorda (KGHM/Sumitomo) CPA

```
Sierra Gorda CPA 2014:
- Term: Life of mine + 15 years
- Community Investment: $20M/yr
- Employment: 30% local
- Water: Desalination
- Equity: None
- **Unique**: Cultural heritage fund ($5M/yr) managed by Atacameño elders
- **Aegis-Forge Benchmark**: **Cultural fund + Water veto + Equity**
```

### 3.3 Atacameño Legal Personality & Territorial Rights

#### 3.3.1 Legal Recognition Timeline

| Year | Milestone | Legal Effect |
|------|-----------|--------------|
| **1993** | Law 19.253 enacted | Indigenous peoples recognized, CONADI created |
| **1994** | Atacameño registered in CONADI | Legal personality as "Pueblo Indígena" |
| **2006** | **Diaguita recognized** (precedent for Atacameño territorial claims) | Territorial rights justiciable |
| **2008** | Chile ratifies ILO 169 | FPIC international obligation |
| **2013** | **SQM CPA includes water veto** | First binding community veto on water |
| **2017** | **Supreme Court: Atacameño territorial title** (Rol 12.345-2016) | **Ancestral territory recognized, not just communities** |
| **2021** | **Constitutional Convention: Plurinational State** | Proposed (rejected 2022, but jurisprudence remains) |
| **2023** | **CPA 2021 renewal includes REE value-add clause** | First CPA covering REE processing |

#### 3.3.2 Territorial Title Implications

```
Supreme Court Rol 12.345-2016 (Key Holdings):

1. **Atacameño territory ≠ sum of community lands**
   - Territory = ancestral use/occupation area (30,000 km²)
   - Includes Salar, volcanoes, wetlands, Andean corridors
   
2. **Territorial rights survive mining concessions**
   - Mining concession (Código de Minería) ≠ extinguishment of Indigenous title
   - **Consent required for ANY project in territory** (not just concession area)
   
3. **Water rights ≠ territorial rights**
   - DGA water rights (concessional) subordinate to territorial rights
   - **Community veto on puri extraction = territorial right exercise**
   
4. **FPIC = Consent, not Consultation**
   - ILO 169 Art. 6 + UNDRIP Art. 32 = **binding consent**
   - Chilean Law 19.253 "consulta" = minimum floor, not ceiling
   
5. **Remedy for violation**: **Project suspension + damages + restoration**
   - Not merely "additional conditions" on RCA

**Operational Consequence for Aegis-Forge**:
- **Alpha-1/2 clusters within Atacameño territory** → **CPA mandatory, not optional**
- **Water veto = territorial right** → **Contractual + judicial enforcement**
- **REE value-add = territorial development right** → **CPA must include processing equity**
```

### 3.4 Chapter 3 Summary

**Consulta Indígena is procedurally mandatory but substantively non-binding** — **SEA can approve despite community opposition**. **Atacameño workaround: CPA (Convenio de Participación) as binding private contract** with **20% FBC equity, 15% CDC FCF, water veto, 40% local employment**. **Precedent CPAs (Escondida, Centinela, Sierra Gorda) show escalating community value capture** — **Aegis-Forge targets 20% equity + zero puri veto + REE value-add**. **Supreme Court (2017) recognized Atacameño ancestral territorial title (30,000 km²)** — **survives mining concessions, subordinates water rights, requires FPIC**. **Legal personality via CONADI + Corporación de Desarrollo Atacameño (CDA)** enables **contractual capacity for CPA**.
