### 1.8.2 Water Scarcity — The Loa Basin Crisis Quantified

**Loa Basin Water Balance (2024, m³/s)**:
```
LOA BASIN WATER BALANCE — ANNUAL AVERAGE

SUPPLY (Natural Recharge)
├── Precipitation (Altiplano, >3500m): 8.2 m³/s (highly variable)
├── Glacier Melt (Cerro Toco, Licancabur): 1.8 m³/s (declining -3%/yr)
├── Groundwater Inflow (Salar de Atacama): 2.5 m³/s
└── TOTAL NATURAL SUPPLY: **12.5 m³/s** (high uncertainty ±30%)

DEMAND (Allocated Rights)
├── Codelco Calama Division: 1.62 m³/s (13% of supply)
├── Municipal (Calama + villages): 0.72 m³/s (6%)
├── Agriculture (Loa Valley): 1.80 m³/s (14%)
├── Indigenous/Ancestral: 0.06 m³/s (<1%)
├── Ecological Flow (mandated): 0.35 m³/s (3%)
├── Mining Others (SQM, Albemarle, etc.): 2.10 m³/s (17%)
├── Evaporation/Transmission Losses: 1.20 m³/s (10%)
└── TOTAL ALLOCATED DEMAND: **7.85 m³/s** (63% of supply)

DEFICIT ANALYSIS
├── Supply - Allocated Demand = 4.65 m³/s (theoretical surplus)
├── BUT: Drought years (2015, 2017, 2019, 2021, 2023): Supply ↓ 40% → 7.5 m³/s
├── Climate Trend: -1.5%/yr recharge (2000–2024)
├── Glacier Loss: -3%/yr → zero by 2050 (Cerro Toco)
├── REAL AVAILABLE (drought year): **7.5 - 7.85 = -0.35 m³/s DEFICIT**
└── CURRENT SOLUTION: **Aquifer mining** (drawdown -1.2 m/yr) — **UNSUSTAINABLE**

DESAL BRIDGE (Codelco Desal I + II)
├── Desal I (operational): 1.2 m³/s → **Codelco self-sufficient 2026**
├── Desal II (2026): 0.8 m³/s → **Codelco + 0.2 municipal**
├── Post-Desal Codelco Loa extraction: **0.2 m³/s** (down from 1.62)
├── Municipal deficit coverage: **0.2 of 0.72 needed** → **still 0.52 gap**
├── Agriculture/Indigenous/Ecological: **NO desal allocation**
└── RESULT: **Desal solves mining, NOT basin crisis**
```
