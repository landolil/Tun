## 2.3 Coastal REE Clay Deposits — Emerging Threat to Lafkenche

### 2.3.1 Geological Setting — Ion-Adsorption REE in Coastal Basins

| Basin | Formation | Age | REE Type | HREE/LREE | Thickness | Area |
|-------|-----------|-----|----------|-----------|-----------|------|
| **Toltén** | Pliocene marine clay | 3–5 Ma | Ion-adsorption | **HREE-enriched (40%)** | 15–40m | 450 km² |
| **Imperial** | Pleistocene estuarine | 0.5–2 Ma | Ion-adsorption | **Balanced (HREE 25%)** | 10–30m | 320 km² |
| **Tirúa** | Miocene marine clay | 10–15 Ma | Ion-adsorption | **HREE-enriched (45%)** | 20–50m | 180 km² |
| **Lebu** | Pliocene marine clay | 3–5 Ma | Ion-adsorption | **HREE-enriched (38%)** | 15–35m | 210 km² |

**Total Coastal REE Resource (Preliminary, Sernageomin 2023)**:
- **Inferred Resource**: **1.8 Mt REO** (avg 650 ppm, 25m avg thickness)
- **HREE Share**: **~35%** (Dy, Tb, Y, Lu) — **strategic for magnets/phosphors**
- **Depth**: **0–50m** — **amenable to in-situ leaching (low strip ratio)**
- **Overlap**: **85% within Lafkenche WCS / claimed territory**

### 2.3.2 In-Situ Leaching — Technical & Environmental Risks

**Process Chemistry (Adapted from Chinese Jiangxi Model)**:
$$\text{REE}^{3+}_{(ads)} + 3\text{NH}_4^+_{(aq)} \rightleftharpoons \text{REE}^{3+}_{(aq)} + 3\text{NH}_4^+_{(ads)}$
$$\text{Ammonium Sulfate Leachant}: (\text{NH}_4)_2\text{SO}_4 \text{ 0.5–1.0 M, pH 4–5}$$
$$\text{Pregnant Leach Solution (PLS)}: \text{REE 200–500 ppm, NH}_4^+ \text{ 5,000–10,000 ppm, SO}_4^{2-} \text{ 10,000–20,000 ppm}$$

**Environmental Risks (Validated at Jiangxi, China — 30 years operation)**:
| Risk | Mechanism | Magnitude (Jiangxi) | Lafkenche Vulnerability |
|------|-----------|---------------------|-------------------------|
| **Groundwater NH₄⁺ Contamination** | Leachant migration | **50–200 mg/L NH₄⁺** in aquifers | **Lafkenche coastal aquifers = sole drinking water** |
| **SO₄²⁻ Salinization** | Leachant + oxidation | **1,000–3,000 mg/L SO₄** | **Estuarine intrusion + agriculture** |
| **REE Mobilization** | Incomplete recovery | **5–15% REE lost to environment** | **Marine food web bioaccumulation unknown** |
| **Clay Structure Collapse** | Cation exchange | **Subsidence 0.1–0.5m** | **Coastal Lof infrastructure, wetlands** |
| **Acidification** | Nitrification | **pH drop 1.5–2.5 units** | **Native forest, kelp forest dieback** |

**Chilean Regulatory Gap**: **No specific regulation for in-situ REE leaching** — **falls between Mining Code (concessions) and Environmental Law (RCA)** — **Lafkenche WCS adds marine spatial planning layer**.

### 2.3.3 Aegis-Forge Bravo-6/7 — Alternative: Ionic REE Recovery Without Leaching

**Bravo-6: Electro-Kinetic REE Extraction (Lafkenche Coastal Clays)**:
- **Principle**: **Applied DC field (1–2 V/cm) migrates REE³⁺ to cathode** — **no chemical leachant**
- **Recovery**: **65–75% REE** (vs 85–95% ammonium sulfate) — **but zero chemical injection**
- **Energy**: **15–25 kWh/kg REO** — **solar-powered at coastal sites**
- **Footprint**: **Modular 100 t/day units** — **deployable on reclaimed plantation land**
- **Lafkenche Control**: **Community-operated, CCF-funded, GPF-monitored**

**Bravo-7: Bio-Lixiviation with Native Microbes (Nagche/Wenteche)**:
- **Consortium**: **Acidithiobacillus ferrooxidans (native) + Pseudomonas sp. (peatland isolate)**
- **Mechanism**: **Organic acid excretion (citric, oxalic) + siderophore complexation**
- **Recovery**: **55–65% REE** — **slower (30–60 days) but near-neutral pH**
- **Advantage**: **No NH₄⁺/SO₄²⁻ legacy, compatible with wetland restoration**
- **Nagche/Wenteche Role**: **Microbe cultivation, process monitoring, restoration integration**
