### 5.2 Mischmetal Production & Applications

#### 5.2.1 Mischmetal Composition & Production

```
Mischmetal (Typical Composition):
- Ce: 45-50%
- La: 22-25%
- Nd: 15-18%
- Pr: 5-6%
- Other REE: 3-5%
- Fe: <5% (from reduction)

Production (Molten Salt Electrolysis):
- Feed: REE chloride (from Stage 8 LREE raffinate after Pr/Nd split)
- Electrolyte: LiF-NaF-KF + REE fluoride
- Cathode: Liquid mischmetal (continuous tap)
- **OPEX: $8-12/kg** (vs $15-20/kg Chinese)
```

### 5.3 LaNi₅ MH Battery Supply Chain

```
LaNi₅ Anode Production:

1. La₂O₃ (Aegis-Forge, 4N, Fe<5ppm, Ni<10ppm)
   │
   ▼
2. La Metal Production (Molten salt electrolysis, Chile)
   La₂O₃ → LaF₃ → La metal (99.9%, O<50ppm)
   │
   ▼
3. LaNi₅ Alloy Synthesis (Japan/US/EU battery mfr):
   La (99.9%) + Ni (99.99%) → Induction melt (1,400°C, Ar)
   Stoichiometry: La:Ni = 1:5.0-5.1
   Ingot → Crushing → Milling (D50=10-20μm) → Classification
   │
   ▼
4. Electrode Fabrication:
   LaNi₅ powder + Binder (PTFE/SBR) + Conductive carbon
   Coating on Ni foam/current collector
   Drying → Calendering → Slitting
   │
   ▼
5. Cell Assembly (NiMH):
   Anode: LaNi₅H₆ (pre-charged)
   Cathode: NiOOH
   Separator: Polyolefin
   Electrolyte: 6M KOH + LiOH
   Casing: Steel (AA, C, D, prismatic)
   │
   ▼
6. Formation & Aging:
   Charge/discharge cycling (3-5 cycles)
   Capacity sorting, sealing
   │
   ▼
**End Markets**: Toyota Prius (15M+), Honda Insight, Ford Fusion Hybrid, industrial UPS, telecom backup

Chilean La Advantage: **4N La₂O₃ → low Fe/Ni impurities → high cycle life, low self-discharge**
```

### 5.4 Ce-Al and Mg-Ce Alloys

#### 5.4.1 Ce-Al Alloys (High-Temperature)

```
Ce-Al System:
- Al₁₁Ce₃ (stable, high melting point 620°C)
- Eutectic: 10% Ce, 560°C
- **Ce 5-10% in Al** → **creep resistance to 300°C** (vs 150°C for 6xxx series)

Applications:
- **Automotive**: Cylinder heads, pistons (high-temp strength)
- **Aerospace**: Engine components (non-rotating)
- **Electronics**: Heat sinks (thermal conductivity 180 W/m·K)

Chilean Ce Role: **Ce metal 99.5% → Ce-Al master alloy (20% Ce) → dilution to 5-10%**
```

#### 5.4.2 Mg-Ce Alloys (Creep Resistance)

```
Mg-Ce System:
- Mg₁₂Ce (stable, high melting point)
- **Ce 1-3% in Mg** → **creep resistance to 200°C** (vs 120°C for AZ91)
- **Grain refinement**: Ce forms Mg₁₂Ce nucleants

Applications:
- **Aerospace**: Gearbox housings, transmission cases
- **Automotive**: Steering wheels, seat frames (lightweighting)
- **Electronics**: Laptop chassis, camera bodies

Chilean Ce Role: **Primary Ce source for Mg-Ce master alloy**
```
