THESEUS	Now, fair Hippolyta, our nuptial hour	
THESEUS	Draws on apace. Four happy days bring in	
THESEUS	Another moon. But, O, methinks how slow	
THESEUS	This old moon wanes! She lingers my desires	
THESEUS	Like to a stepdame or a dowager	
THESEUS	Long withering out a young man’s revenue.	
		
HIPPOLYTA	Four days will quickly steep themselves in night;	
HIPPOLYTA	Four nights will quickly dream away the time;	
HIPPOLYTA	And then the moon, like to a silver bow	
HIPPOLYTA	New -bent in heaven, shall behold the night	
HIPPOLYTA	Of our solemnities.	
THESEUS	Go, Philostrate,	
THESEUS	Stir up the Athenian youth to merriments.	
THESEUS	Awake the pert and nimble spirit of mirth.	
THESEUS	Turn melancholy forth to funerals;	
THESEUS	The pale companion is not for our pomp.	
THESEUS	Philostrate exits.	
THESEUS	Hippolyta, I wooed thee with my sword	
THESEUS	And won thy love doing thee injuries,	
THESEUS	But I will wed thee in another key,	
THESEUS	With pomp, with triumph, and with reveling.	
		
		Enter Egeus and his daughter Hermia, and Lysander
